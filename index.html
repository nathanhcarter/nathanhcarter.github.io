About Me

While I was born in South Carolina, growing up in a military family meant that a lot of my early years were spent moving across the United States. Through all of these moves and changes, two things stayed constant: my love for games and my passion for learning new things.

While I initially expected to become a veterinarian, even shadowing at clinics as a child, my path took a detour through the arts. I spent nine years as a saxophonist, earning various honors and intending to pursue music professionally. However, during my time at James Madison University, I discovered the beauty of Mathematics. My experiences in early math courses marked a turning point, transforming a subject I once wrote off into a lifelong calling.

After working as a Software and DevOps Engineer, I realized that my true passion lies at the intersection of theory and application. I have since presented research both within and outside of JMU, and I am now dedicated to pursuing a life in academia and advanced mathematics. I believe that quantitative rigor can unlock new insights into the world that many may not recognize as possible.

When I’m not deep in a proof or researching, you can find me tutoring students in music and math, volunteering, or spending time with my girlfriend. I am a firm believer in the power of lifelong learning and am always looking for the next complex problem to solve.

Future Plans: Research in Applied Mathematics

I plan to pursue graduate studies and research in applied mathematics, focusing on how continuous modeling, optimization, and dynamical systems can be applied to complex real-world phenomena.

Areas of Interest:

  • Applied optimization, continuous algorithms, and numerical analysis.
  • Search theory and optimal search strategies for target detection and location problems.
  • Applied optimization techniques and numerical algorithms for physical and ecological systems.
  • Flexible quantitative modeling across physical, computational, and natural systems.

While I remain deeply interested in search theory and computational modeling, I aim to build a strong foundation across deterministic and stochastic methods before selecting a specific dissertation topic. I am excited to explore new subfields in applied math and build tools that translate across disciplines.
